Which resolution should you choose?

Higher resolution means a bigger file and a longer wait. These are typical sizes for a ten-minute video, though the real size depends on how much movement is in the footage.

OptionResolutionRough size (10 min)Best for
360p640 × 360~40 MBSlow connections, talking-head videos
480p854 × 480~70 MBPhone viewing, saving data
720p HD1280 × 720~150 MBThe everyday balance
1080p Full HD1920 × 1080~250 MBLaptops and TVs
1440p 2K2560 × 1440~600 MBLarge monitors
2160p 4K3840 × 2160~1.5 GB4K screens, editing footage

Only the resolutions that the uploader actually published appear in the dropdown. If a channel uploaded in 720p, no tool anywhere can produce a genuine 1080p file from it — anything claiming otherwise is upscaling and re-encoding, which makes the video look worse.

What happens after you press Download

  1. The video stream and the audio stream are fetched separately, which is how YouTube serves high-resolution content.
  2. FFmpeg merges them into a single MP4 without re-encoding, so there is no quality loss and no watermark.
  3. The title and channel are written into the file's metadata.
  4. The finished file is sent to your browser and saved to your device automatically.

Supported links

  • youtube.com/watch?v=… — standard video pages
  • youtu.be/… — short share links
  • youtube.com/shorts/… — see the Shorts downloader
  • music.youtube.com/… — best paired with MP3 conversion
  • youtube.com/live/… — after the stream has ended
